# Voice

Read this before writing any headline, subhead, feature name or button label.
Measured across apple.com product pages: **median headline length is 3 words**,
the longest run to 14, and 42% end with a full stop despite not being sentences.

## The shape of a headline

A declarative fragment, ending in a period.

> Semua jenis kekuatan.
> Lebih mudah untuk Anda.
> Capai potensi membaca maksimal Anda.
> M5. M5 Pro. M5 Max.

Not a sentence, not a question, not a slogan with an exclamation mark. The
period is doing the work: it makes a fragment land like a statement of fact.
A headline with no terminal punctuation reads unfinished; one with an
exclamation mark reads like an advertisement, which is the thing Apple's voice
is built to avoid.

## Rules

1. **Three words is the target.** Seven is long. If it needs more, it is two
   headlines, or it is a headline and a subhead.
2. **End with a full stop**, even on a fragment.
3. **One idea per headline.** If there is an "and" in it, consider splitting.
4. **Say the benefit, not the specification.** The spec belongs in the subhead
   or the feature list. "Pintar. Aman. Langsung di perangkat." — the chip name
   is nowhere in it.
5. **Repetition with a period is a rhythm device.** "M5. M5 Pro. M5 Max."
   Three beats, three stops. Use it for a product family or a tiered feature.
6. **No exclamation marks. No questions in headlines** — questions belong in
   support and FAQ contexts, where Apple does use them, deliberately.
7. **No superlative stacking.** One claim per line. "Terbaik di dunia" works
   because the rest of the sentence is plain.

## Eyebrows

The small line above a headline is a category or a product name, not a teaser.
"Performa dan Kekuatan Baterai", "Mac + iPhone". No period on an eyebrow — it
is a label, not a statement.

## Subheads

One sentence, and this one *is* a sentence. It carries the concrete detail the
headline withheld: "Peredam Kebisingan Aktif dalam telinga, terbaik di dunia."
Around 6–14 words.

## Buttons

Two words at most. A verb, or a verb plus a noun: "Selengkapnya" (Learn more),
"Beli" (Buy), "Bandingkan" (Compare). Never "Click here", never a sentence,
never a trailing period. Apple pairs exactly two actions — one filled, one
outlined — and the filled one is the one it wants you to take.

## Feature names

Title Case, short, and treated as proper nouns once introduced. They are not
described in the name — "Dynamic Island" tells you nothing about what it does,
which is the point: the name is a handle, the copy underneath does the
explaining.

## Typographic details

- Bind product names with `&nbsp;`: `iPhone&nbsp;18&nbsp;Pro`,
  `MacBook&nbsp;Air`, `macOS&nbsp;27`. They must never wrap mid-name.
- Use real typographic characters: curly quotes, en dashes in ranges, and `×`
  for dimensions rather than the letter x.
- Footnotes are numbered superscripts tied to a legal block at the page
  bottom, set in 12px caption with `--ink-muted`.

## What this voice is not

It is not playful, not clever, and not conversational. It does not use "we", it
rarely uses "you" in headlines, and it never makes a joke. Confidence comes
from brevity and the full stop, not from enthusiasm. When a draft feels like it
is trying, cut it in half and put a period on it.
